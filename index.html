Cinematic drone

A fast, nimble 5 inch deadcat built for chasing and filming moving cars. The deadcat layout pushes the front arms wide so the props stay out of the GoPro’s view.

The frame is fully 3D printed in carbon fiber filled nylon (PAHT-CF). It runs 6S with 1750 KV motors, a Darwin stack, a Caddx Ratel 2 analog camera and an iFlight BLITZ VTX. A GoPro HERO13 Black sits on top, and the mount takes the camera in landscape or vertical orientation.

5 inchDeadcat cinematic drone

Simulation

Due to the unconventional motor layout, I created a mod for the drone flight sim Liftoff FPV that lets me input my own geometry and motors. This helped me get used to the unusual longitudinal moments. It was also very useful when making changes to the frame or avionics, since I could feel how they affected the flight dynamics. It also let me create a preliminary custom PID tune.

High speed drone launcher

2026 to Present In development

As Launch System Engineer with CU Boulder SuperSpeed, I’m designing a portable bungee launch system for a high speed drone targeting speeds above 200 mph.

The design has been through multiple versions as revisions were made, some big, some small. The design is now closed and parts are being ordered and machined.

18 m/sRequired launch velocity at an 8° launch angle

The problem

CU SuperSpeed’s first high speed drone, CUSS1, weighs about 9.5 kg. Because it was designed for 200 mph steady flight, it has no control authority below 40 mph, so it needs a launch system that is also portable and easy to assemble.

The solution

Many teams tackle this problem with a launcher whose bungee cord is anchored far ahead of the launch rail. Our design is self contained, meaning all of the bungee length is onboard. That is harder to build, but it solves several issues the conventional design has. Our solution routes the bungee in and out of the central cavity of the extrusion rail with a multi pulley system to increase the effective bungee length.

Launch calculator

To size the bungee and braking cords, I built a launch calculator. It models the nonlinear stretch of the bungee cord along with aircraft thrust, friction and the rail incline, then steps through the launch to find the release speed and simulates the braking stroke after release. Cord sizes and counts can be changed and saved, which made it quick to compare layouts as the design changed.

Quadcopter motor test stand

2026 to Present In development

A low cost bench for measuring the thrust of small brushless FPV motors and propellers. The motor mounts on a printed platform directly above a 5 kg S-type load cell, so thrust is read axially.

An ESP32 reads the load cell through an amplifier board and commands the motor through a 60 A ESC. Throttle control and data logging run from my laptop.

5 kgS-type load cell

Status

Parts were ordered and manufactured in September 2026 and the stand is assembled. Wiring and logging software are in progress.

Purpose

Measured thrust curves will replace catalogue estimates in my drone models, including the flight dynamics tools I use to tune the cinematic drone’s motor geometry.

CU Boulder Robotics / Aerial SurveillanceBack to work

Surveillance quadcopter

2026 to Present In development

As a Drone Engineer at CU Boulder Robotics, I’m designing a small quadcopter that flies above the competition field, locates the opposing team’s robots and relays their positions to our team in real time.

Flying at 5 m, the camera can see about 66% of the field at once. That lets the drone hold one position over the arena and track most of the robots without chasing them around.

From lidar to camera

The first concept carried a lidar array sized to cover more than 60% of the field from most hover locations. The current design replaces the lidar with a camera for spotting and tracking robots.
