Cinematic drone

A fast, nimble 5-inch deadcat built for chasing and filming moving cars. The deadcat layout pushes the front arms wide so the props stay out of the GoPro’s view.

The frame is fully 3D-printed in carbon-fiber-filled nylon (PAHT-CF) on a Bambu Lab P1S and designed in Onshape. It runs 6S with 1750 KV motors, a Darwin stack, a Caddx Ratel 2 analog camera and an iFlight BLITZ VTX. A GoPro HERO13 Black sits on top, and the mount takes the camera in landscape or vertical orientation.

5-inchDeadcat cinematic drone

Simulating the geometry first

Because a deadcat’s motors aren’t symmetric about the center of gravity, I wrote MATLAB tools to compare motor layouts using mass, CG, inertia and thrust, then extended them to hover trim, control authority, PID response and full 6DOF simulation. To feel the geometry before building it, I made a Liftoff simulator mod that flies the custom layout with a RadioMaster Pocket.

High-speed drone launcher

2026 — Present In development

As Launch System Engineer with CU Boulder SuperSpeed, I’m designing a portable bungee launch system for an electric ducted fan (EDF) drone targeting speeds above 200 mph.

The design has gone through five versions, from a PVC pipe frame to an aluminum extrusion rail on a braced stand, with a wheeled carriage and bungee runs on both sides of the rail.

18 m/sRequired launch velocity at an 8° launch angle

How it works

The carriage rides V-groove wheels along an aluminum extrusion rail set at an 8° incline. Bundled ¼-inch MIL-C-5651 bungee cords run around pulleys at the front of the rail and pull the carriage forward; a second set of braking cords picks up near the end of the stroke. The aircraft separates when the carriage begins to decelerate, and the carriage is reset with a hand winch.

Launch calculator

To size the cords I built a launch calculator that models nonlinear bungee force from the MIL-C-5651 spec points, aircraft thrust, rolling friction and incline. It steps through the launch stroke, finds the release point, then simulates braking and rebound, with selectable cord sizes and counts and saved configurations.

Quadcopter motor test stand

2026 — Present In development

A low-cost bench for measuring the thrust of small brushless FPV motors and propellers. The motor mounts on a printed platform directly above a 5 kg S-type load cell, so thrust is read axially with no lever arm to calibrate out.

An ESP32 reads the load cell through an amplifier board and commands the motor through a 60 A ESC, with support for 6S packs. Throttle control and data logging run from my laptop.

5 kgS-type load cell

Status

Parts were ordered and manufactured in September 2026 and the stand is assembled. Wiring and logging software are in progress.

Purpose

Measured thrust curves will replace catalogue estimates in my drone models, including the flight dynamics tools I use to tune the cinematic drone’s motor geometry.

Surveillance quadcopter

2026 — Present In development

As Surveillance Drone Engineer at CU Boulder Robotics, I’m designing a small quadcopter that flies above the competition field, locates the opposing team’s robots and relays their positions to our team in real time.

The airframe wraps every propeller in a continuous printed guard ring and lands on a curved skid, so it can survive contact with robots, walls and the floor in a crowded arena.

≤250 gTarget all-up mass

From lidar to camera

The first concept carried a lidar array sized to cover more than 60% of the field from most hover locations. The current design replaces the lidar with a camera for spotting and tracking robots.
